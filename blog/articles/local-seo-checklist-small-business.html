A Simple Local SEO Checklist Any Small Business Can Do This Week

The handful of local search basics that make the biggest difference, none of which require hiring an agency.

Local SEO gets talked about like it is complicated. For a single-location or single-area service business, most of it is not. Here is the short list that covers the bulk of it.

Claim and fully fill out your Google Business Profile

Business name, category, service area, hours, phone number, and at least a few real photos. An incomplete profile is a common reason a legitimate business gets outranked by a competitor who simply finished setting theirs up.

Keep your name, address, and phone number identical everywhere

Your Google Business Profile, your website footer, your Facebook Page, and any directory listing (Yelp, Angi, Nextdoor, and the like) should list your business name, address, and phone number exactly the same way. "123 Main St" in one place and "123 Main Street Suite A" in another is a small inconsistency that search engines treat as a signal something might be off.

Say your city and service area on your own website, in plain text

Not just in a logo or an image. Actual readable text on the page: the towns or counties you serve, spelled out. A search engine cannot read text baked into a photo.

Get reviews, and get them regularly

Review count and review recency both matter for local ranking, and they matter even more for whether a customer actually calls once they find you. A steady trickle of new reviews beats a pile of old ones that stopped three years ago.

Make sure your site actually works on a phone

Most local searches happen on a phone, and a site that is hard to use on one loses customers regardless of how it ranks. Pull it up on your own phone and see whether the phone number is one tap away.

That is the list

No agency retainer, no keyword tool, no monthly report required to do any of this. It takes an afternoon to get the basics right, and most local competitors have not bothered to.
