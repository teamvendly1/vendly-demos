A Website Checklist for Local Service Businesses That Actually Matters

The handful of things worth fixing on a small business website, and the long list of things you can safely ignore.

Most small business owners either have no website or have one that has not been touched since it was built. Both are fixable, and neither requires the redesign you have been putting off. Here is what to actually check, in the order it matters.

1. Your phone number is clickable and correct

Open your own site on your phone and tap the phone number. If it does not dial, that is costing you calls today. Check every page it appears on, including the footer, because a stale number from an old redesign is more common than you would think.

2. It loads fast on a phone, on a bad connection

Most of your visitors are on a phone, often standing in a driveway or a parking lot with weak signal. A homepage stuffed with huge, unoptimized photos is the single most common reason a small business site feels slow. Compress images before uploading them, and if you are not sure how, most website platforms do this automatically now, so check whether yours already handles it.

3. Real photos of your actual work, not stock

A generic stock photo of a smiling person in a hard hat does nothing for you. A real photo of a job you actually completed, even an average phone photo, builds more trust than a polished stock image because it looks like proof rather than decoration.

4. Your service area and hours are stated plainly

Do not make someone guess whether you serve their town or whether you are open on Saturdays. Say it in plain text near the top of the page, not buried in a paragraph.

5. There is exactly one clear thing to do next

Call now, text now, or request a quote. Pick one primary action and make it the most visible button on every page. A page with five competing buttons (book online, chat, email, download our brochure, follow us on Instagram) usually gets fewer of any of them than a page with one obvious one.

What you can skip

  • A blog you will not keep updating (an empty or abandoned blog looks worse than no blog at all)
  • Animated intros or video backgrounds that slow down the page
  • A built-in live chat widget if nobody on your team will actually answer it
  • Matching every design trend you saw on someone else's site

A plain, fast, honest site with a working phone number will out-earn a beautiful one that is slow or hides the phone number. Fix the five things above first, then worry about the rest.
