How Often You Should Actually Update Your Business Website

What genuinely needs regular attention on a small business site, and what is fine to leave alone for years.

A lot of business owners either never touch their site again after launch or feel like they should be redesigning it every year. Neither is right. Some things need checking constantly. Most things do not need to change at all once they are correct.

Check monthly: the facts that go stale

  • Hours, especially around holidays
  • Phone number and service area, if either has changed
  • Pricing or service list, if you have added or dropped anything
  • Any seasonal promotion that has already ended but is still showing

These are the things that actively mislead a customer if they are wrong, so they deserve a five-minute check on a regular basis, not a full redesign.

Update as it happens: proof of your work

New photos from a completed job, a new review worth featuring, a new certification or license. Add these as they happen rather than batching them up, because a site that visibly reflects recent work reads as an active business rather than one that put up a page once and walked away.

Leave alone unless something is actually broken: the design itself

A layout, color scheme, or font choice does not need to change just because it has been a while. Customers are not comparing your site's design against last year's version. They are deciding, in a few seconds, whether you look legitimate and whether they can find your phone number. If the current design already does that, redesigning it is time spent on something that was not actually a problem.

The real signal that it is time for a bigger change

Not a calendar date. A change in your business: you added a service line the current site does not mention, you moved locations, or you keep hearing from customers that they could not find something obvious. Let the business drive the update schedule, not the other way around.
