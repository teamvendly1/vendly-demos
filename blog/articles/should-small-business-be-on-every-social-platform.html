Do You Really Need to Be on Every Social Platform

Why picking one or two platforms and actually showing up beats spreading yourself thin across all of them.

It is common advice to tell a small business to be everywhere: Facebook, Instagram, TikTok, LinkedIn, X, YouTube, all at once. For a one or two person operation, that advice usually leads to five accounts that all look abandoned instead of one that looks alive.

An abandoned account is worse than no account

A profile with a post from eight months ago tells a visitor the business might not be around anymore, or is not paying attention. If you cannot keep a platform updated at least a couple times a month, you are usually better off not creating the account at all.

Go where your actual customers already are

A visual trade with obvious before-and-after results (landscaping, remodeling, detailing, painting) tends to do well on Instagram or Facebook, where a photo carries the whole message. A B2B service selling to other business owners often gets more out of LinkedIn than out of a platform built around personal photos. Pick based on where the people who hire you actually spend time, not based on which platform is trending.

One platform, done consistently, beats four done occasionally

Posting real work regularly on a single platform builds a following that recognizes your business. Spreading the same limited time across four platforms usually means every one of them gets updated rarely enough that nobody builds that familiarity anywhere.

Expand only once the first one is working

Once one platform is genuinely running (regular posts, real engagement, an occasional lead coming from it) is the right time to consider a second, not before. Adding platforms to a marketing plan that is not working on one yet just spreads the same problem thinner.
