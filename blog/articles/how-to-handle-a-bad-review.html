How to Handle a Bad Review Without Making It Worse

A level-headed way to respond to a negative review, and the responses that tend to backfire.

Every business that has been around long enough gets a bad review eventually. What you do with it matters more than the review itself, because the person reading it later is not just judging the complaint. They are judging how you handled it.

Wait before you respond

A review that stings is easy to answer badly in the first ten minutes. Read it, close the tab, and come back to it later in the day. Almost every response written the next morning is better than the one written in the heat of the moment.

Do not argue the details in public

Even if the review gets a fact wrong, a public back-and-forth about who said what makes both sides look worse and gives the disagreement a permanent, searchable home. Acknowledge the complaint, keep your reply short, and take the specifics to a phone call or a direct message.

A response that works

"Sorry to hear that, this is not the experience we want anyone to have. Please give us a call at [number] so we can make it right." That is it. No defensiveness, no essay, no listing out everything the customer supposedly did wrong.

If the review is fake or clearly not a real customer

You can flag it for removal through your Google Business Profile if it violates their policies (it is not about a real experience with your business, it contains no actual visit, or it is spam). This takes time and is not guaranteed, so do not count on it as your only plan. A calm, professional public reply still protects you while you wait.

One bad review rarely sinks anything

A single low review sitting among a steady stream of genuine, recent good ones reads as normal, because everyone knows no business bats a thousand. What actually hurts is silence: a bad review with no reply at all, sitting there unanswered for anyone to find.
