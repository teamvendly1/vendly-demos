Facebook Ads vs Google Ads for Local Trades: How to Pick Where to Start

A plain-language look at how the two platforms actually work for a local trade business, and how to decide which one to try first.

Both platforms can work for a local trade business. They work in different ways, and picking the wrong one first is a common way to waste a small ad budget before you learn anything useful.

Google Ads catches people already looking

Someone searching "emergency plumber near me" or "roof repair [your town]" already has a problem and is actively looking for someone to solve it right now. Google Ads puts you in front of that exact search. This is why Google tends to work well for services people search for when something breaks: plumbing, HVAC, towing, locksmiths, and similar urgent, need-it-now categories.

Facebook and Instagram ads create the want

Nobody is searching for "deck builder" on Facebook. Instead, a well-targeted ad, especially one with a real before-and-after photo of your own work, shows up in front of someone who was not actively looking but recognizes the problem when they see it ("our deck looks exactly like that before photo"). This tends to work better for services that are more discretionary or planned in advance: remodeling, landscaping, painting, fencing, and similar projects people consider rather than urgently need.

If you can only start with one

Ask what kind of decision your customer is making. If they search for you when something is already broken, start with Google. If your best customers are people who did not know they wanted the work done until they saw a good example of it, start with Facebook or Instagram.

Either way, the ad is only half the job

An ad that sends someone to a slow site with no clear phone number, or to a Facebook Business Page with no recent posts and no reviews, wastes the click you paid for. Fix the landing page and the review count before spending real money on ads pointed at either one.

Start small and watch what actually happens

Run a small budget on one platform for a couple of weeks before committing more. Track actual calls and quote requests, not just clicks. Clicks tell you the ad got attention. Calls and booked jobs tell you whether it made money.
